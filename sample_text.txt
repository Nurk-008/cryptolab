Every day our university network carries thousands of private messages: grades, passwords, payment details and personal chats. If these messages travel as plain text, anyone who can listen to the network can read them. Encryption turns readable text into a secret code that only the person with the right key can turn back. Old ciphers like Caesar and Vigenere were used for hundreds of years, but today a normal laptop can break them in less than a second, because they keep the patterns of the language. Modern ciphers such as AES hide these patterns completely, so the only way in is to guess the key, and there are far too many keys to try.
